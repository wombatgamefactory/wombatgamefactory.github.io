Eject Four

Available to License

Connect. Eject. Protect.

Players 2
Play Time 15 Min
Age 6+
Teach Time 1 Min
Components 26 Pieces + Frame

Connect Four has been selling on the same one-line promise since 1974. Get four of yours in a row before the other player gets four of theirs. Eject Four keeps that promise word for word, then adds the move every player has wanted to make since they were six years old: you can knock the other player's piece out and stand in its place.

The reason that is a game and not a mess is that ejecting is rationed. Each player gets thirteen pieces and no more - eight ordinary, four ejectors, and one yellow. Ejectors are the only thing that can remove an ordinary piece. The yellow is the only thing that can remove an ejector, and nothing at all can remove a yellow. Whatever you knock out goes straight back to its owner, so every attack spends a scarce piece of yours to hand them ammunition back. Begin a turn with an empty supply and you lose on the spot. Players work that out in about four turns, stop swinging, and start setting traps.

The teach is one sentence, because most of it is already done: same as Connect Four, but you can knock mine out. Sixty seconds to explain, fifteen minutes to play, and a skill ceiling that keeps climbing long after a six year old has learned to block. It is the rare two-player box a parent and a child can share honestly, and that two adults will still ask for a rematch on.

How It Plays

  1. Place. On your turn, put one of your pieces into any empty space touching a piece already on the frame, diagonals included. A neighbour of any colour counts.
  2. Or eject. Instead of placing, spend one of your four ejectors to knock out an opponent's ordinary piece and take that space for yourself.
  3. Break an ejector. Spend your single yellow piece to knock out an opponent's ejector. Yellow pieces can never be ejected by anyone and count towards nobody's line, so they stay on the frame as permanent blockers.
  4. Give it back. Every ejected piece returns to its owner's supply. Attacking always costs you more than it costs them.
  5. Win. Four of your pieces in a row wins immediately, in any direction, ordinary and ejector pieces mixed freely. Start a turn with nothing left in your supply and you lose.

Why It Belongs On That Shelf

  • Nothing to teach. The win condition is the win condition a shopper already knows, so the box does the explaining and the new rule fits on the back panel.
  • One new move. Everything else is a rule the buyer learned decades ago. The whole design is a single addition, carefully priced.
  • Two players, ages 6+, fifteen minutes. Parent and child, or two adults, out of the same box, with a genuine handicap built into the supply if you want one.
  • No language on any component. Twenty-six pieces and a frame. One worldwide artwork, translation limited to the leaflet.
  • Low bill of materials. Two player colours plus yellow, three piece sculpts, one frame, one leaflet. Everything moulds.
  • Built as a line, not a box. Frame size, supply size and eject cost all tune independently, and have been tested across 6x6, 7x7 and 8x8 grids. That supports a travel edition, a standard edition and a deluxe or licensed edition off one rules set.
  • Frame shape is free. The prototype is 3D printed as a tree, but the geometry underneath is a plain grid and will take whatever silhouette a brand team wants.
  • Ready now. Rules are settled and playtested, a physical prototype exists, and a free browser version runs with no install for remote evaluation.

Eject Four is unpublished and unencumbered, and is offered for licence. Dean Morris is a London-based designer with three published titles. A prototype can be shipped, or demonstrated in person in the UK or at a convention. Get in touch at dean@wombatgamefactory.com.
