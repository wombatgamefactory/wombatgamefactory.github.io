Ned Kelly

In Development

Aim just under the iron.

Players 2–5
Play Time 20–30 Min
Age 12+
Cards 72
Status In Development

Ned Kelly is a trick-taking game in which every player is a trooper in the cordon round the Glenrowan inn, and the target is a figure in an iron suit. The leader fires blind. Then a Ned card is turned to show the line: how high you can aim. The best shot at or under the line hits Ned and takes his card and its reward. The shot just over it rings off the iron and into the inn. The biggest share of the reward wins.

Two cards matter in every trick. The highest card at or under the line scores, in any suit, while the lowest card over it risks the inn: its player turns an Inn card, and four of the twelve are a Hostage hit that costs £300. The lead is a bet, because you fire before the line is known, and whoever hit Ned must fire blind again. Hostage hits stay face up, so everyone can see what is left in the Inn deck.

Night runs into dawn. Early lines are low and pay £100, so a careful low shot finds Ned. At dawn he walks into the open: the lines rise to 12 and the rewards climb to £300, so save your 10s, 11s and 12s. Four suits, Train, Lantern, Gun and Fire, name the cards, but once they are down any suit can hit.

The game has 72 cards: 48 trooper cards in four suits numbered 1 to 12, 12 Ned cards (six Night and six Dawn) and 12 Inn cards (eight misses and four Hostage hits). Score on paper. It plays with two to five players in about 20 to 30 minutes, ages 12 and up. It is a playable prototype with a full deck and rule book.

Glenrowan, 1880. On 28 June 1880 the Kelly gang held the Glenrowan inn, and at dawn a figure in home-made iron armour walked out of the mist towards the police cordon. The game puts you in the cordon, firing into the dark, with the reward on his head and the inn behind him full of people.
