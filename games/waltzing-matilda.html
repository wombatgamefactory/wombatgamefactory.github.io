Waltzing Matilda

In Development

Steal the sheep. Beat the troopers.

Players 2–5
Play Time 20–30 Min
Age 10+
Cards 55
Status In Development

Waltzing Matilda is a trick-taking game built on the Australian bush song. In it the trump suit is never dealt. The trumps are the squatter's sheep, twelve jumbucks that wait by the billabong, and you steal them by winning tricks.

The top jumbuck always sits face up, a sheep come down to drink. Win a trick and you may grab it into your hand, where it becomes your best trump. The catch is that you cannot lead a jumbuck, so you can only get rid of one when you cannot follow suit. Three Trooper cards are dealt into the hands and can be played at any time. Each one raises the stakes, and the third ends the hand on the spot.

Then comes the reckoning. Every jumbuck still in your hand costs you a point for each Trooper on the line, three points if the third Trooper ended the hand. A jumbuck is both your strongest card and the evidence against you, so every grab is a gamble on whether you will be void in time.

The game has 55 cards in four suits (Yellow, Green, Red and Blue, marked by a billy, a windmill, a bush hat and a black swan), plus a Billabong card and a score pad. It plays with two to five players, best with three or four, in about 20 to 30 minutes, with one hand dealt per player. It is a concept being playtested.
