Snowy River

In Development

High wins the climb. Low wins the descent.

Players 2–5
Play Time 20–30 Min
Age 10+
Cards 58
Status In Development

Snowy River is a trick-taking game that turns upside down at the summit. It is based on Banjo Paterson's poem The Man from Snowy River (public domain), and every hand follows the poem in order. A prize colt has run off with the wild horses, and the mob is the prize.

Up the mountain, the highest card of the led suit wins each trick, and the mob climbs a step for every trick, and one more for every off-suit card. At the summit everyone chooses in secret: Chase the mob and stake your climb tricks on bringing it home, or Watch and keep them. Then comes the terrible descent, where the lowest card wins. The weedy pony that could never win a climb trick suddenly takes the ride down.

Every card has two lives. A 12 rules the climb and is useless on the way down, and a 1 is the reverse. You set the clock too: run out of a suit and the mob climbs faster, flipping the order before the big hands are spent. If nobody chases, the mob escapes and is worth more next hand.

The game has 58 cards: 48 horse cards in four suits numbered 1 to 12 (Escape, Climb, Descent and Home), a two-sided Mountain card, the Colt, four Brumby cards and five Chase and Watch cards, plus a mob marker and a score pad. It plays with two to five players in about 20 to 30 minutes, one hand each. It is a concept being playtested.
